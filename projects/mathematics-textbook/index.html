MATHEMATICS TEXTBOOK

A calm, step-by-step digital mathematics textbook focused on integration, with worked examples and clear chapter navigation.

01. The Idea

The Idea

First-time learners often meet integration as a wall of formulas. The idea was a quieter digital textbook: pick a chapter, open a section, and walk through worked examples one move at a time instead of scanning a dense PDF.

02. What I Built

What I Built

I built a React and Vite learning interface around integration topics, with chapter and section navigation, step-by-step examples, and a reading layout meant to stay calm on both desktop and phone. The demo is live on Cloudflare Pages.

  • Chapter and section navigation
  • Step-by-step worked examples
  • Integration-focused chapters
  • Calm reading layout
  • Responsive study interface
  • Cloudflare Pages deployment
03. User Experience

User Experience

The path is chapter, then section, then one example at a time. That keeps attention on the current algebraic move instead of forcing the learner to hold an entire method in their head while scanning ahead.

04. Design Decisions

Design Decisions

The design favors quiet type, generous spacing and a steady reading rhythm. Decoration and chrome stay secondary so formulas and explanations remain the main visual weight on the page.

05. Technical Approach

Technical Approach

Built with React and Vite as a static front-end app. Content is organized by chapter and section in the client, which keeps navigation fast and makes it simple to add another worked example without redesigning the page shell.

06. SEO Perspective

SEO Perspective

The case study page holds the searchable title, description and project schema, while the live demo focuses on the learning experience. Clear project naming and a stable demo URL keep the work easy to find from the portfolio.

07. What I Learned

What I Learned

Educational interfaces fail when they show everything at once. Breaking methods into chapter, section and single-step examples made the content feel lighter without cutting the math. The hard part was sequencing, not styling.
