Three things I check before I publish a site

By Agon Mustafaj · October 11, 2026

A site can look finished on my computer and still not be ready for a visitor. Before I publish it, I run through three short checks. They do not take long, and they have saved me from mistakes that show up immediately.

The title should say what the site is

The browser title is the first line someone sees in Google. If it is only a business name, the visitor cannot tell whether they will find the service they need. I write it so it reads clearly: who you are and what you offer.

It has to be readable on a phone without zooming

I open the site on a narrow screen. If the text is cut off, a button slips under the menu, or a photo covers the call to action, I fix that before publishing. Most visitors arrive on a phone.

Contact should be one step, not a search

At the end I ask myself: if I wanted to call or write today, would I find the way immediately? The form, WhatsApp, or phone number needs to be visible. A good-looking site that never leads to contact leaves the visitor halfway there. These three points are my short list. The design can change. These checks stay.
